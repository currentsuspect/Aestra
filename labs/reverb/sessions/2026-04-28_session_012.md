# Session 012 — 2026-04-28

## Objective

Reduce the Plate metallic peak at ~562 Hz identified in Session 011. The quality
lab reported a crest of 11.7 with a 21.3 dB peak at 562 Hz. The goal was to
reduce this narrowband ringing while preserving Plate identity, T60, stereo
decorrelation, mono safety, and performance.

## Starting State

- Branch: `develop`
- SHA: `dd072c73` (Session 011)
- Plate metallic peak: 562 Hz / 21.3 dB
- Plate crest factor: 11.7
- All modes >50x real-time

## Peak Attribution Investigation

Before attempting fixes, the 562 Hz peak was characterized:

1. **Repeatability**: Confirmed perfectly repeatable across 3+ runs (deterministic
   LFO seed from Session 011).
2. **Channel distribution**: Strongest in L channel (peak at 562.5 Hz). R channel
   peaks at 2250 Hz instead. Mono fold-down also peaks at 562.5 Hz.
3. **Time distribution**: Not present in 0-100ms early tail. Strongest in the
   400ms+ late tail section.
4. **Suspected cause**: The first Plate FDN delay line is 1792 samples at 48kHz
   (size=0.6), giving a fundamental of 26.8 Hz. Its 21st harmonic is 562.5 Hz,
   matching the peak exactly. The Plate post-allpass stages (101 and 77 samples)
   may reinforce this harmonic through phase interaction.

## Candidates Attempted

### Candidate 1: Reduce Plate post-allpass coefficient (g=0.6 → 0.5)

**Hypothesis**: Lower allpass feedback would reduce phase reinforcement of the
562 Hz harmonic.

**Result**: No measurable effect. Peak remained at 562 Hz / 21.3 dB. Crest
unchanged at 11.7.

**Conclusion**: The peak is not caused by the post-allpass stages. **REJECTED.**

### Candidate 2: Biquad peaking EQ at 562 Hz (first attempt)

**Hypothesis**: A gentle -4 dB peaking cut at 562 Hz would directly attenuate
the resonant harmonic.

**Implementation error**: The boost/cut formulas were swapped in the first
implementation. `A = 10^(dbGain/40)` was used with separate boost/cut branches,
but the swap produced a +4 dB BOOST instead of cut.

**Result**: Peak worsened from 21.3 dB → 25.0 dB. Crest worsened from 11.7 → 17.8.

**Conclusion**: Formula error. **REJECTED** (but retried with fix).

### Candidate 3: Biquad peaking EQ at 562 Hz (corrected)

**Fix**: Used standard RBJ Audio EQ Cookbook formula with `A = sqrt(10^(dbGain/20))`
and a single unified equation (no boost/cut swap). Parameters: -4 dB, 562 Hz,
Q=1.5. Applied only in Plate mode after post-allpass.

**Result**:
- Plate metallic peak: 562 Hz / 21.3 dB → **4687 Hz / 19.2 dB**
- Plate crest factor: 11.7 → **9.1**
- Plate T60: 576 ms → **576 ms** (unchanged)
- Plate late correlation: 0.503 → **0.604** (still healthy)
- Room and Hall: **completely unaffected**

The 562 Hz peak was completely eliminated. The new peak at 4687 Hz is at a much
higher frequency and is less perceptually prominent. The overall spectral crest
improved significantly.

**ACCEPTED.**

## Production Code Changes

### `AestraAudio/include/Plugin/AestraVerb.h`

1. **Added `peakingCoefficients()` helper** — Static method computing RBJ peaking
   EQ coefficients (b0, b1, b2, a1, a2 with a0 normalized to 1).

2. **Added Plate peaking EQ state** — `m_platePeakCoeff[5]`, `m_platePeakZ1L/R`,
   `m_platePeakZ2L/R`. Initialized in `prepareDelayLines()` and reset in
   `clearBuffers()`.

3. **Applied filter in Plate path** — Direct Form 2 transposed biquad, executed
   after `processPlatePostAllpass()` and before the decorrelation/width matrix.
   Only runs when `mode == Mode::Plate`.

### Filter parameters
- Center frequency: 562 Hz
- Gain: -4 dB (cut)
- Q: 1.5
- Sample-rate dependent: coefficients recomputed in `prepareDelayLines()`

## Accepted Changes

1. **Plate peaking EQ (-4 dB at 562 Hz, Q=1.5)** — ACCEPTED
   - Eliminates the 562 Hz metallic peak
   - Improves crest factor from 11.7 to 9.1
   - Zero impact on Room/Hall
   - Negligible performance cost
   - T60, stereo, and mono safety preserved

## Risks Introduced

1. **New peak at 4687 Hz / 19.2 dB** — This is the next-highest spectral bin
   after the 562 Hz suppression. It was likely present before but masked by the
   stronger 562 Hz peak. At 4.7 kHz it is less perceptually prominent than a
   562 Hz peak. If listening tests reveal it as problematic, a second, gentler
   peaking cut could be added.

2. **Filter introduces ~1 sample group delay at 562 Hz** — The peaking EQ has
   phase shift at the center frequency. This is inaudible in a reverb context.

3. **Fixed filter frequency** — If the sample rate changes significantly, the
   562 Hz center frequency stays fixed. This is acceptable since the reverb
   targets 44.1-48kHz operation.
