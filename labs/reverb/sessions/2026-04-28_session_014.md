## Root Cause Analysis

### Why Was Density 0?

1. **Silent first 50ms**: The quality lab analyzed the first 50ms after the
   impulse, but AestraVerb's predelay and processing latency mean the first
   significant energy appears at ~53-60ms. The first 50ms is essentially silent.

2. **Threshold too high**: The old metric used `directPeak * 0.02` (2% of the
   input impulse peak = 0.02 absolute). The actual early reflection energy is
   in the range 0.05-0.40, so the threshold was within range, BUT the first 50ms
   window completely missed the early energy.

3. **Single burst, not multiple peaks**: AestraVerb's early reflection network
   produces a concentrated energy burst at ~54-60ms, not multiple discrete
   peaks spread across the first 50ms. The old metric assumed multiple peaks.

### Signal Characteristics

| Mode | First Non-Zero | Peak Value | Peak Time |
|------|---------------|-----------|-----------|
| Room | 54.3 ms | 0.381 | 54.3 ms |
| Hall | 59.9 ms | 0.190 | 59.9 ms |
| Plate | 53.1 ms | 0.081 | 76.9 ms |

Hall is particularly sparse: after the initial spike at 60ms, it goes nearly
silent until the FDN tail builds up. Room has a large spike and then continuous
energy. Plate has a gradual build-up with the peak at 77ms.

## New Metric Design

Replaced the single `earlyDensity` field with a comprehensive `TransientMetrics`
struct containing 11 fields:

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| `earlyOnsetMs` | First sample > -60 dB relative to early-window peak |
| `earlyPeakMs` | Time of maximum in 0-150ms window |
| `earlyPeakCount` | Local maxima above -30 dB rel to early-window peak |
| `earlyActiveBins1ms` | 1ms bins with energy above -40 dB |
| `earlyActiveBinRatio` | Active bins / total bins |
| `earlyCrestFactor` | Peak / RMS in early window |
| `earlyEnergyMs25` | ms to reach 25% of cumulative early energy |
| `earlyEnergyMs50` | ms to reach 50% of cumulative early energy |
| `earlyEnergyMs75` | ms to reach 75% of cumulative early energy |
| `earlyDensityScore` | Composite: `peakCount * activeRatio / crestFactor` |

### Key Design Decisions

1. **Window: 0-150ms** — Wide enough to capture the actual early energy burst
   (~54-77ms onset) while staying within the "early reflection" perceptual window.

2. **Threshold relative to early-window peak** — Not the input impulse peak.
   This makes the metric sensitive to the actual early reflection energy level.

3. **Mono sum for analysis** — `abs(L) + abs(R)` captures both channels' energy.

4. **Composite density score** — More peaks + more active bins = higher density.
   Divided by crest factor to penalize sparse single-spike responses (like Hall).

## Results

### Before vs After

| Mode | Old Density | New Onset | New PeakCount | New ActiveRatio | New Crest | New DensityScore |
|------|------------|-----------|---------------|-----------------|-----------|------------------|
| Room | **0** | 54.3 ms | 593 | 0.55 | 26.9 | **12.07** |
| Hall | **0** | 59.9 ms | 29 | 0.14 | 40.8 | **0.10** |
| Plate | **0** | 53.1 ms | 1375 | 0.65 | 9.8 | **90.54** |

### Interpretation

**Hall (0.10)** — Very sparse early response. Single large spike at 60ms, then
near-silence. This is correct for a hall reverb: spacious, with distant early
reflections.

**Room (12.07)** — Moderate density. Large spike at 54ms followed by continuous
energy. The active ratio of 0.55 means energy is spread across about half the
early window.

**Plate (90.54)** — Very dense/diffuse. The allpass/diffuser network creates
fine-grained energy throughout the early window. The peak count of 1375 reflects
high-frequency ringing in the early response. The crest factor of only 9.8
(vs 26.9-40.8 for Room/Hall) confirms that Plate's early energy is spread out
rather than concentrated in a single spike.

### Energy Buildup Comparison

| Mode | 25% Energy | 50% Energy | 75% Energy |
|------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Room | 59.5 ms | 92.1 ms | 109.3 ms |
| Hall | 59.9 ms | 65.4 ms | 83.4 ms |
| Plate | 79.0 ms | 98.3 ms | 117.4 ms |

Hall reaches 50% energy fastest (65.4 ms) — its energy is concentrated in the
initial spike. Plate is slowest (98.3 ms) — energy is diffuse and builds
gradually. Room is in between.

## Risks Introduced

1. **Peak count may be noisy** — High-frequency ringing (especially in Plate)
   creates many local maxima. The peak count alone is not a robust metric;
   it should be interpreted alongside active ratio and crest factor.

2. **150ms window is arbitrary** — Some reverb designs may have early energy
   outside this window. The metric is specific to AestraVerb's architecture.

3. **Density score formula is heuristic** — `peakCount * activeRatio / crestFactor`
   is a rough composite. It may need tuning based on listening tests.
