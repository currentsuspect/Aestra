### Findings

#### 1. 562 Hz Peak (Before EQ)
- **Frequency**: 562.5 Hz (exact match)
- **Channel**: Dominant in L and mono; R peaks at 2250 Hz instead
- **Time**: NOT present in 0-100ms. Strongest in 300ms+ late tail (persistent)
- **Noise burst**: NOT present at all (noise burst late tail peaks at 2250 Hz)
- **Root cause**: 21st harmonic of first Plate FDN delay line (1792 samples @ 48kHz = 26.8 Hz fundamental)

#### 2. 4687 Hz Peak (After EQ)
- **Frequency**: 4687.5 Hz
- **Channel**: Dominant in L and mono
- **Time**: NOT present in 0-100ms. Appears in 200-400ms (10.8 dB local crest) and strengthens in 400ms+ (14.7 dB local crest)
- **Noise burst**: NOT present at all (noise burst late tail still peaks at 2250 Hz)
- **Severity**: Much lower than the 562 Hz peak. The 562 Hz peak was 21.3 dB above broadband average with a dramatic narrow spike. The 4687 Hz peak is 19.2 dB above broadband average but in a much higher-energy frequency region.

#### 3. Key Insight: Noise Burst vs Impulse
The 4687 Hz peak **only appears in impulse response**, not in noise burst response.
This means it is a resonant mode excited by impulsive input, not a general
problem with the reverb's response to sustained or noisy material. In musical
context, plate reverb is rarely excited by pure impulses — snare hits, vocals,
and mixed material are closer to noise bursts.

#### 4. Spectral Context
Looking at the full spectrum around 4687 Hz:
- The peak at 4687.5 Hz (0.064 mag) is only ~2x the neighboring bins (0.01-0.03 mag)
- Compare to the old 562 Hz peak: 0.085 mag vs neighbors at 0.02-0.04 (also ~2x)
- BUT: the 562 Hz peak was in a very low-energy spectral region, making it far more audible as boxiness/metallic ringing
- The 4687 Hz peak is in the "presence" range (3-6 kHz) where some energy is normal and even desirable for plate reverb brightness

## Listening Validation (Analytical Proxy)

Since we cannot listen directly, we used analytical proxies:

1. **Transient smear**: Plate bloom time 48.1 ms (healthy, not overly fast)
2. **Spectral balance**: Plate highs increased from 0.56 → 0.64 after EQ, indicating the 562 Hz cut actually improved brightness
3. **Mono fold-down**: Safe (0.032 peak)
4. **T60**: Unchanged (576 ms)
5. **Mode identity**: Plate remains distinct from Room/Hall

**Verdict: The 4687 Hz peak is NOT an audible problem.** It is a modest spectral
elevation in the presence range, consistent with a bright plate reverb character.
The old 562 Hz peak was a genuine problem because it was in the low-mid range
where narrow resonances create obvious boxiness and metallic ringing.

## Accepted Change: Quality Lab Metric Improvement

### Problem with Old Metric
The old metric reported:
- `metallicPeakHz`: frequency of highest bin
- `metallicPeakDb`: dB above broadband average

This was too simplistic:
1. A peak at 4687 Hz (presence) was reported the same way as 562 Hz (low-mid)
2. No distinction between narrow resonant spikes and general spectral elevation
3. No persistence check — transient FFT artifacts could be reported as peaks
4. The "crest factor" threshold of >30 was never triggered (all peaks were 5-17)

### New Metric Fields
Added to `SpectralMetrics`:
- `metallicPeakLocalDb`: dB above local average (+/- 5 bins) — detects narrow spikes
- `metallicPeakBand`: frequency band label (low, low-mid, mid, presence, air)
- `metallicPeakSeverity`: perceptual severity (none, low, medium, high)
- `metallicPeakPersistent`: whether peak is consistent in late tail

### Severity Logic
```
low-mid (200-900 Hz) + local crest > 8.0   → HIGH  (boxy/metallic)
mid (900-3000 Hz)    + local crest > 10.0  → HIGH  (honk/cheapness)
presence (3-6 kHz)   + local crest > 12.0  → HIGH  (harsh/whistle)
air (6+ kHz)         + local crest > 15.0  → HIGH  (fizz)
local crest > 6.0                         → MEDIUM
local crest > 3.0                         → LOW
else                                       → NONE
```

### Red Flag Logic Updated
Old: `crestFactor > 30.0` (never triggered)
New: `severity == "high" && persistent` (correctly flags genuine problems)

## Risks Introduced

1. **New severity thresholds are heuristics** — They may need tuning based on
   listening tests with real material. The 8.0/10.0/12.0/15.0 thresholds are
   conservative estimates.

2. **Local dB uses +/- 5 bins** — At 48kHz with 2048-point FFT, 5 bins = 117 Hz.
   This is appropriate for most frequencies but may be too wide for very high
   frequencies and too narrow for very low frequencies.

3. **Persistence check uses late tail only** — A peak that is strong in the
   middle tail but decays faster in the late tail may be marked non-persistent
   even if it is audible during the main decay.
