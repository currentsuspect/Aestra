## New Tool: AestraReverbMaterialLab

A new standalone executable that:
1. Generates 6 synthetic dry signals:
   - **Snare**: 50ms white noise + 200Hz body tone
   - **Bright ping**: 2kHz sine burst with fast decay
   - **Vocal phrase**: 220Hz fundamental + harmonics + vibrato + envelope
   - **Chord stab**: C-major chord (261/329/392/523 Hz) with fast ADSR
   - **Low pulse**: 80Hz sine with slow decay
   - **Mix bus**: Superposition of kick, snare, and pad
2. Renders each signal through AestraVerb in Room, Hall, and Plate modes
3. Writes dry and wet WAV files
4. Computes metrics for each rendered output:
   - Peak, RMS, crest factor
   - Spectral balance (low/mid/high ratio via difference-magnitude proxy)
   - Late correlation
   - Mono peak and tail RMS
   - Metallic peak severity
5. Generates JSON and Markdown reports

## Results & Analysis

### Tail Energy Comparison (tailRMS)

| Source | Room | Hall | Plate | Plate/Room |
|--------|------|------|-------|-----------|
| Snare | 0.0132 | 0.0153 | **0.0025** | 19% |
| Bright ping | 0.0223 | 0.0201 | **0.0027** | 12% |
| Vocal phrase | 0.5360 | 0.3452 | 0.4023 | 75% |
| Chord stab | 0.1057 | 0.0934 | **0.0427** | 40% |
| Low pulse | 0.0123 | 0.0204 | **0.0046** | 37% |
| Mix bus | 0.0781 | 0.0597 | **0.0380** | 49% |

**Key finding:** Plate produces dramatically lower tail energy on transient
material (snare, bright ping) than Room/Hall — only 12-19% of Room's tail RMS.
On sustained material (vocal phrase), Plate is much closer to Room (75%).

This suggests Plate mode may sound thin or short on percussion and plucked
instruments, even though its impulse-response T60 is 576ms. The difference is
likely because plate reverbs diffuse energy very quickly, and transient material
doesn't have enough sustained energy to maintain the tail.

### Stereo Width (late correlation)

| Source | Room | Hall | Plate |
|--------|------|------|-------|
| Snare | -0.333 | 0.501 | 0.587 |
| Bright ping | 0.262 | 0.428 | -0.154 |
| Vocal phrase | -0.264 | 0.501 | -0.373 |
| Chord stab | -0.826 | 0.824 | -0.404 |
| Low pulse | 0.886 | 0.796 | 0.253 |
| Mix bus | -0.915 | 0.813 | -0.522 |

**Hall** maintains consistently positive late correlation (~0.5-0.8), indicating
a stable, moderately wide stereo image.

**Room** shows strong negative correlation on chord stab (-0.826) and mix bus
(-0.915), indicating very wide stereo — possibly too wide for some material.

**Plate** shows mixed results: positive on snare (0.587), negative on vocal
(-0.373) and mix bus (-0.522). The Session 011 decorrelation (k=0.60) may be
interacting differently with different frequency content.

## Key Findings

1. **Plate transient tail energy is very low** — On snare and bright ping,
   Plate tail RMS is only 12-19% of Room. This may cause Plate to sound thin
   on percussion. The impulse-response T60 of 576ms is not representative of
   the perceived tail length on transient material.

2. **No metallic problems on any material** — All modes and sources show
   "low" metallic severity. Sessions 012-013 fixes are confirmed effective.

3. **Room stereo may be too wide** — Negative late correlation (-0.826 to
   -0.915) on chord stab and mix bus suggests the Session 011 decorrelation
   (k=0.60) might be excessive for dense harmonic material.

4. **Hall is the most consistent mode** — Healthy late correlation (0.5-0.8)
   across all sources, moderate tail energy, balanced spectrum.

5. **Vocal phrase clipping is expected** — Sustained input + reverb feedback
   naturally exceeds 1.0 with mix=1.0. Not a bug.

## Risks Introduced

1. **Synthetic signals are not real music** — These are simplified approximations.
   Real snare, vocal, and chord recordings may behave differently.

2. **Spectral balance uses a rough proxy** — The difference-magnitude method
   for estimating low/mid/high ratios is not a true spectral analysis. It should
   be interpreted qualitatively, not quantitatively.

3. **Tail RMS depends on input duration** — Shorter inputs (snare 500ms) have
   less time to build up tail energy than longer inputs (vocal 1s). Cross-source
   comparisons should account for this.

If (b), consider:
